About The Eye of the Sahara

Discover the fascinating facts and mysteries behind this natural wonder.

The Richat Structure

The Eye of the Sahara, also known as the Richat Structure, is a prominent circular geological feature in the Sahara desert near Ouadane, west-central Mauritania. This incredible formation measures approximately 40 kilometers (25 miles) across and is visible from space, making it a notable landmark for astronauts.

Initially interpreted as an asteroid impact crater, the Eye is now widely thought to be a deeply eroded geological dome. The formation consists of alternating layers of sedimentary, metamorphic, and igneous rocks that were pushed upward in a dome-like structure and then eroded over time.

Formation Process

Scientists believe that the Richat Structure formed when a pocket of magma pushed up from deep within the Earth but did not break through to the surface. Instead, it caused the layers of rock above to bulge upward, creating a dome. Over millions of years, erosion wore away the top and exposed the circular pattern of rock layers we see today.

The age of the Richat Structure is estimated to be between 485 and 400 million years old (Late Ordovician to Early Devonian period). The rocks exposed in the center of the structure are much older than those on the rim, which is consistent with the dome erosion theory.

Fascinating Features

What makes the Eye of the Sahara so special

Concentric Rings

The structure features a series of concentric circles that are made up of different types of rock, creating a natural bullseye in the desert.

Unique Ecosystem

Despite being in one of the world's harshest deserts, the structure harbors different plant species than the surrounding area due to its unique geology.

Astronaut Landmark

Early space missions used the Eye as a landmark, with astronauts noting its perfect circular shape and distinctive appearance from orbit.

Scientific Significance

The Eye of the Sahara provides geologists with a rare opportunity to study the exposed layers of Earth's crust in a way that's not commonly found elsewhere. Its unique circular pattern allows scientists to examine rock formations that would normally be hidden deep underground.

Alternative Theories

While the geological dome theory is most accepted, other hypotheses have been proposed, including the possibility that it was formed by the impact of a meteorite that was later eroded, or that it was created by a massive upheaval of magma from below that never quite reached the surface.

The structure has also become important for researchers studying desertification processes, as the changing environment around the Eye offers insights into how the Sahara has evolved over time.

Visitor Information

Planning a visit to this natural wonder

Location

The Eye of the Sahara is located in Mauritania, West Africa, approximately 30 kilometers away from the town of Ouadane.

Best Time to Visit

The ideal time to visit is between November and February when temperatures are more moderate. Summer temperatures can exceed 50°C (122°F).

Travel Advisory

Due to its remote location, visiting requires careful planning. A guided tour with experienced desert travelers is highly recommended.
